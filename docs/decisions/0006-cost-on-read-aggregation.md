# 0006. Cost-on-read aggregation

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-05

## Context

The frontend needs kWh and cost per hour, per day and per month. The database holds kWh per UTC hour
([0005](0005-energy-integration.md)). The price of a kWh depends on the band of the hour (valle, llano or
punta), on the holidays, and on the tariff in force on that date ([0002](0002-versioned-tariffs-in-yaml.md)).
All of these can be corrected or can change later, and the stored history must survive that.

Days and months are local concepts. A plug owner's day starts at local midnight, which is not a UTC hour
boundary, and in a time zone with daylight saving time a local day can have 23 or 25 hours.

## Decision

We will compute cost when it is read (`consumption` in `app/aggregation.py`).

- **Price every stored hour on its own.** Each row gets the tariff of its local day and the band of its
  local hour. A day or a month is the sum of its hourly costs, never total kWh times one rate.
- **Buckets are local hours, days and months.** `make_buckets` builds them for the local dates
  `[from, to)`; months snap to whole months. Hour buckets are generated by stepping in UTC, so a daylight
  saving day has 23 or 25 of them and no two share a start.
- **Limits on the range**: 8 days of hours, 62 days or 36 months per request, so one request cannot ask
  for an enormous response. Beyond that, `RangeError`.
- **Round once, at the end.** Totals add the exact values and are rounded once (6 decimals for kWh, 4 for
  pesos), so they do not drift. They can differ from the sum of the rounded buckets by a rounding unit.
- **Missing data is reported, not hidden.**
  - Hours before the first tariff keep their kWh but have no cost, and the response carries a `no_tariff`
    warning with the first affected day.
  - A year with no holiday list carries a `missing_holidays` warning, because its holidays would be priced
    as business days.
- **The response is typed and strict.** `consumption` returns a Pydantic model (`ConsumptionResponse`).
  Models are frozen and reject unknown fields. The two warning kinds are a discriminated union on `code`,
  so a warning can never mix the fields of the other kind.
- **`now` is a parameter**, not read inside the function. It decides which bucket is still in progress
  (`partial`), and tests can fix it.

## Alternatives considered

- **Storing cost in the hourly table**: it freezes today's tariff into old rows, and a correction in the
  YAML files would not reach the history.
- **Pricing a day as total kWh times an average rate**: wrong, because the band changes during the day.
- **Aggregating in SQL with `GROUP BY`**: the band depends on holidays and on the tariff version, which
  live in Python. Doing it in SQL would need those tables in the database as well.
- **One `warnings` model with optional fields**: simpler, but it allows inconsistent combinations such as
  a holiday warning without a year.
- **Plain dictionaries as the return value**: less code, but nothing checks their shape, and mypy cannot
  help.

## Consequences

- Reading a month loads at most about 31 x 24 rows per outlet, which is fine for a home installation.
  A much larger scale would need caching or a summary table.
- Fixing a tariff or a holiday fixes the whole history at once.
- The 2027 holidays are not in `config/holidays.yaml` yet, so requests that touch 2027 return a
  `missing_holidays` warning until they are added.
- The JSON keys of `outlets` and `totals` are outlet ids, which JSON turns into strings (`"1"`). The
  frontend must know this.
- The `no_tariff` field is called `since`, not `from`, because `from` is a Python keyword.
